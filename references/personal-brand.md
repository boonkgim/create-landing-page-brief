# Archetype: Personal brand

A page where **the person is the thing being trusted**. Consultant, coach, creator, founder, freelancer, speaker, advisor.

## What's different

The visitor is not evaluating a product, they are deciding whether to believe a person. That changes three things:

- **The differentiator is point of view, not mechanism.** Ten consultants offer the same service. What separates them is a distinctive, arguable belief about how the work should be done. If the user can't state theirs, keep digging in the interview until they can — it is the single highest-leverage answer on the page.
- **Credibility is cumulative, not a single proof point.** Body of work, clients, media, results, tenure. It has to be shown, not claimed.
- **The visitor wants to know who they'd be working with.** A business page can skip the origin story. This one usually can't.

## Common failure modes

- **Résumé instead of argument.** The page lists what the person has done, in their own frame, and never names the visitor's problem. Fix: the hero is about the visitor's outcome, not the person's title.
- **Diluted ask.** Book a call, subscribe, read the blog, follow, buy the course, all at equal weight. Pick one primary. Everything else is a footer link.
- **Vague positioning.** "I help people grow." Push for the specific person and the specific change.
- **Modesty tax.** The user underclaims real credentials. Pull the specifics out in the interview and put them in.

## Section stack

Changes from the default are marked. Order matters; this order is the argument.

1. **Hero** — name, who you help, what changes for them, one CTA. Not a job title. The outcome, in the visitor's language.
2. **Proof bar** — *adapted:* as-seen-in, client logos, stages spoken on, audience size, years in the field, notable results. Whichever is strongest and true.
3. **Problem** — the audience's pain, in their words. Unchanged in job.
4. **Point of view** — *new, replaces "Solution / How it works" as the trust step.* The arguable belief: what most people in this field get wrong, and what you do instead. This is where a personal brand is won. It must be specific enough that a competent peer could disagree with it.
5. **How we'd work together** — *adapted from "Solution":* the three steps of engagement, from first contact to result. Kill the ambiguity of "what actually happens if I reach out."
6. **Benefits / what changes** — outcomes for them, not your capabilities.
7. **Body of work** — *replaces "Features / proof of mechanism".* Clients, projects, companies built, writing, talks, products, numbers. The concrete record a skeptic can verify.
8. **Testimonials / case studies** — named people, specific results, verbatim. Strongest immediately before the ask.
9. **About / origin** — *new.* Short. Why this person does this work, and the experience that earned the point of view. Placed here, not at the top: it answers "who am I dealing with" only after they care about the outcome. Cut it if the user has no real story; a padded origin reads as filler.
10. **Why me** — *from "Objection handling".* Direct: who this is for, who it isn't, and what you offer that the alternatives don't. Naming who it's *not* for buys more credibility than any claim.
11. **The ask** — what they get, what it costs (money, or an hour of their time for a call), what happens in the first minute after they act. If it's a call: how long, with whom, what you'll cover, whether there's a pitch.
12. **De-risking** — *usually small here:* no-pitch call, reply anytime, first session free, cancel anytime, unsubscribe in one click. Match it to the currency; don't invent a guarantee where none exists.
13. **FAQ** — the real questions that come up before someone reaches out: availability, price range, remote vs in person, timeline, how it works with their team.
14. **Final CTA** — repeat the hero ask, one strongest reason to act now.
15. **Where else to find me** — *optional, secondary.* Newsletter, social, writing, for the visitor who isn't ready. Keep it visually and hierarchically below the primary ask; never competing with it.

## Interview emphasis

Spend extra questions on:
- The point of view. "What does everyone in your field believe that you think is wrong?"
- The specific person they serve. Push past a segment to a situation: not "startup founders" but "founders who just raised a seed and are hiring their first five people."
- Concrete record. Numbers, names, years, notable clients, publications, results.
- What happens after the click, in detail. This is the highest-anxiety moment on a personal brand page.

# Archetype: Business

A page where **a product or service is sold as the solution to a named problem**. SaaS, app, agency, productized service, physical product, local service, course, workshop.

## What's different

The visitor is evaluating an offer against alternatives, including doing nothing. That changes three things:

- **The mechanism has to be credible.** They need to believe the thing actually works, not just that the outcome is desirable. Specificity is the proof.
- **There is always a status quo competitor.** A spreadsheet, a manual process, an incumbent tool, an in-house hire, or nothing. Name it. A page that doesn't beat the status quo loses to it silently.
- **Ambiguity about price, scope and next step is the top silent killer.** More deals die here than at any claim.

## Common failure modes

- **Feature list as argument.** Specs with no outcome attached. Every feature needs a "so you can…".
- **Generic problem section.** Written in the company's language, not the buyer's. If it could appear on a competitor's page, it's not doing work.
- **Unnamed alternative.** The page never acknowledges what they'd do instead, so it never beats it.
- **Hidden pricing with no reason given.** If price can't be shown, say what determines it and give a range or a starting point.
- **Weak next step.** "Get started" with no indication of what happens, how long it takes, or whether a card is needed.

## Section stack

The default stack, with these specifics:

1. **Hero** — what it is, who it's for, the outcome. Category clear within one line; a visitor should never have to guess what kind of thing this is.
2. **Proof bar** — customer logos, user count, rating with source, one hard result stat.
3. **Problem** — the buyer's pain, verbatim. Name the status quo here if it's the real enemy.
4. **How it works** — three steps, from signup to result. Include the boring operational detail: setup time, what's needed from them, who does what.
5. **Benefits** — outcomes, quantified where possible.
6. **Features / proof of mechanism** — the specifics behind each claim. Each feature paired with the outcome it produces.
7. **Testimonials / case studies** — named, with role and company, and a number. Place the strongest right before pricing.
8. **Objection handling / Why us** — comparison against the real alternative (including "do nothing" and "build it in-house"), or an explicit is-this-for-you / not-for-you.
9. **The ask (offer & pricing)** — tiers or a starting price, exactly what's included, and a "which one is me" line per tier. If pricing is custom, state what drives it and the typical range.
10. **De-risking** — guarantee, free trial, no card required, cancel anytime, money-back window, SLA, pilot.
11. **FAQ** — the five or six real pre-purchase questions from sales and support.
12. **Final CTA** — repeat the ask, with the strongest reason to act now.

## Sub-type adaptations

Apply on top of the stack above, and state what you changed:

- **SaaS** — add *integrations*, *security & compliance*, *migration and onboarding*. Pricing needs tiers plus per-tier fit lines.
- **Agency / high-ticket service** — add *case study deep-dive*, *who we work with (fit criteria)*, *how engagements work* in place of fixed pricing. Add *who you'll actually be working with*, since buyers fear the bait-and-switch of meeting seniors and getting juniors.
- **Workshop / course / cohort** — replace *Features* with **Agenda / curriculum**. Add *who's teaching*, *dates, format, location*, *who should attend and prerequisites*, *what you leave with*.
- **Local service** — add *service area*, *process and timeline*, *licensing and insurance*. Proof must be local and named.
- **Physical product** — add *materials and specs*, *shipping and returns*, *sizing or fit*.
- **B2B enterprise** — add *ROI or business case*, *objections by buying-committee role* (economic buyer, end user, security, procurement), *implementation and support*.
- **Waitlist / pre-launch** — cut pricing. Add *what happens after you join* and *why we're building this*.
- **Lead magnet** — collapse to Hero, Problem, What's inside, Proof, CTA. Anything longer kills the ask.

## Interview emphasis

Spend extra questions on:
- The status quo alternative, by name, and why it fails.
- Numbers: how much time or money saved, how fast, for whom, measured how.
- Exactly what's included at each price, and what isn't.
- What happens in the first five minutes after they click, and whether a card is required.
